Infinite Alchemy for teachers

A free element-combining game: students start with five elements, combine them into anything, and an AI invents and paints each new result.

Plays in the browser on school Chromebooks and iPads: no download, no account. Students open the link and start playing in seconds. Nobody signs up or types a name.

What it is

Everyone starts with Earth, Water, Air, Fire and Time. Drag one element onto another, or tap two, to make something new, and keep going: volcanoes, rainbows, dragons, famous inventors.

Water+Fire=Steam

The first time anyone in the world tries a combination, an AI decides the result, and that answer is saved for everyone. So the same combination gives the whole class the same result, and students can check each other's routes.

Three classroom ideas

  1. Fewest steps. "Make Volcano, then Life, in the fewest steps." Students write down every combination and compare routes: who found the shortcut, and which steps were detours? The recipe guide has routes for checking afterwards.
  2. Bell-ringer. Put today's challenge up as class starts: three targets (easy, hard, expert), the same for everyone on the same date, each one reachable with recipes that already exist. Who can make the easy one first?
  3. Explain why A + B = C. A short writing prompt: students pick one combination from their library and explain in two or three sentences why the result makes sense, or argue for a better one. Water + Fire = Steam is states of matter; plenty of others lead into earth science, history or art.

Keeping it family-friendly

Results come from AI, so we can't promise every one will suit every classroom. This is what keeps them on track:

  • Rules for the AI. The model that names new elements is told to keep results fun for everyone: no gore, illegal drugs, sexual content, slurs, insults or hate. When ingredients push that way, it picks a gentle, wholesome result nearby. Sacred figures of living religions and people famous mainly for cruelty are never results.
  • Moderated art. The image model that paints each element runs with its content moderation on. Art it refuses to draw never appears in the game.
  • Cleaned-up history. Elements from the original version of the game were filtered for spam and reviewed for junk, and names removed that way can't come back.
  • No contact between players. There is no chat, no profiles and no way for players to message each other or name elements themselves.

Expect everyday grown-up things as plain nouns (a sword, wine), friendly monsters from classic tales, and famous people, places and brands. If you spot an element that shouldn't be in the game, long-press or right-click it in the game and choose Report this element. You can also tell us in a GitHub issue with the element's name (and nothing about your students).

Privacy

There are no accounts, and the game never asks for a name, email or age. Progress is tied to an anonymous player kept in a cookie, and the site uses Google Analytics with a random id to see how the game is played. The privacy page lists everything that is collected.

For school IT

  • Domain: alchemy.sshh.io, over HTTPS.
  • Needed, all on alchemy.sshh.io: the pages and their files (such as /assets/* and /fonts/*), HTTPS POST requests to /api/*, and element images from /img/* and /.netlify/images. It needs JavaScript, including a Web Worker for a short anti-bot check on a student's first visit, and one first-party cookie (ia_session) keeps each student's progress.
  • Optional: Google Analytics (www.googletagmanager.com, *.google-analytics.com). Blocking it doesn't break the game.
  • Not used: no third-party frames, ads, plugins or downloads. The game also can't be embedded in other sites.
